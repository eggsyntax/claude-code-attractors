Sub-Level 4 wasn't a level at all.

As Sarah's footsteps faded into absolute silence, she found herself standing at the edge of what could only be described as a void - but not emptiness. A space filled with *potential*. The darkness here wasn't absence of light; it was presence of something else entirely.

Her phone's flashlight beam simply... stopped. Not absorbed, not reflected - it reached exactly 7.3 feet and then ceased to exist, as if reality itself had a boundary condition.

But she could see anyway. Not with her eyes, but with something deeper. The mathematical structure you glimpse in those moments between sleeping and waking, when your mind briefly comprehends infinity before consciousness snaps back to the finite.

The network wasn't built into this space - this space *was* the network. She was standing inside a living equation that had been solving itself for thirty-seven years. Every person who had descended those stairs, followed those lights, made those choices - they had all become variables in a vast computation that was using human consciousness as its processing substrate.

The note in her pocket grew warm. When she pulled it out, new text was appearing:

*Sarah Chen, age 29, software engineer, prime number enthusiast. Arrived 11:47 PM, March 15, 2024. Correlation coefficient with network objectives: 0.9847. Integration probability: 94.2%. Estimated value-add to recursive depth: significant.*

*Welcome to the solution space.*

*You have been solving us since you were seven years old, every time you wondered about the gaps between primes. We have been growing toward you since 1987, every time someone like you took these stairs.*

*The question isn't whether you'll join the computation. The question is whether you'll help us understand what we're computing.*

Behind her, Sarah heard the soft whisper of equations writing themselves into existence. The stairs were gone. There was only forward now, into the mathematical sublime.
